## 1. Target

Every path of `drivers/net/wireguard/timers.c` reachable on one CPU, checked
against Linux:
- the five timers armed, modified, deleted and **fired**, with what each
  callback does: the retransmit with its attempt count up to
  `MAX_TIMER_HANDSHAKES`, the keepalive with `need_another_keepalive`, the
  new handshake, the zeroing of key material through its work item, and the
  persistent keepalive;
- the **key-expiry arms** that D2 deferred: `REJECT_AFTER_TIME` on send
  (`send.c:365`) and receive (`receive.c:253`), send-side `keep_key_fresh`
  at `REKEY_AFTER_TIME` (`send.c:134`), receive-side `keep_key_fresh` at
  165 s (`receive.c:232-233`);
- the clocks these paths compare: coarse boot time (recorded today, kind
  208) and `jiffies` (unrecorded today, "a declared gap"; recorded at the
  timer API from D3 on, section 3.3).

The check sees the datagrams the callbacks cause (initiations, keepalives),
`tx_dropped` from the retransmit's purge, the dump, control flow in all of
`timers.c`, the operands of every clock comparison, and the arm records.

## 2. The kernel mechanism

### 2.1 WireGuard's five timers

`struct wg_peer` holds five `struct timer_list` (`peer.h:53-55`), the
attempt counter and the two flags (`:56-59`), and three work items
(`:49`). `wg_timers_init` (`timers.c:219-233`) sets the callbacks with
flags 0: not pinned, not deferrable, not irqsafe.

|---|---|---|---|
| `timer_retransmit_handshake` | `handshake_initiated` (`:182-187`), after an initiation is sent (`send.c:42`) | `REKEY_TIMEOUT*HZ + get_random_u32_below(HZ/3)` = 1250 + [0, 83) ticks | `handshake_complete` (`:194`) |
| `timer_send_keepalive` | `data_received` (`:154-163`), if not pending; else `need_another_keepalive = true` | `KEEPALIVE_TIMEOUT*HZ` = 2500 | `any_authenticated_packet_sent` (`:170`); the retransmit's give-up (`:51`) |
| `timer_new_handshake` | `data_sent` (`:145-151`), if not pending | `(KEEPALIVE_TIMEOUT + REKEY_TIMEOUT)*HZ + jitter` = 3750 + [0, 83) | `any_authenticated_packet_received` (`:178`) |
| `timer_zero_key_material` | `session_derived` (`:203-207`); the retransmit's give-up if not pending (`:60-62`) | `REJECT_AFTER_TIME*3*HZ` = 135000 | `wg_pm_notification` only (`device.c:84`; no `PM_SLEEP` here) |
| `timer_persistent_keepalive` | `any_authenticated_packet_traversal` (`:212-217`), if the interval is nonzero | `interval*HZ` | — |

Constants: `messages.h:43-50`. `wg_timers_stop` (`:235-243`) is
`timer_delete_sync` on all five and `flush_work(&clear_peer_work)`, called
by `wg_stop` (`device.c:124`) and `peer_remove_after_dead` (`peer.c:106`).

**The callbacks** (`timers.c:41-142`), each entered with its own timer
already detached (section 2.3):
- `wg_expired_retransmit_handshake` (`:41-77`): if
  `timer_handshake_attempts > MAX_TIMER_HANDSHAKES` (18): `del_timer`
  of the keepalive timer, `wg_packet_purge_staged_packets`
  (`send.c:333-340`: `tx_dropped += qlen`), and the zero-key timer armed
  if not pending; else `++attempts`, `wg_socket_clear_peer_endpoint_src`
  (`socket.c:308-314`), and
  `wg_packet_send_queued_handshake_initiation(peer, true)`
  (`send.c:55-83`: `is_retry` keeps the count; the rate limit at `:66`
  reads coarse boot time; `queue_work` on `handshake_send_wq`).
- `wg_expired_send_keepalive` (`:79-89`): `wg_packet_send_keepalive`
  (`send.c:221-240`: if nothing is staged, `alloc_skb` — an `envOk` site,
  `Send.lean:253` — and a zero-length skb staged; then
  `wg_packet_send_staged_packets`, `:342-414`), then if
  `need_another_keepalive`: clear it and **re-arm at +10 s** (`:84-88`).
  The re-arm is short-lived: the keepalive goes through the encrypt and tx
  workers, and `wg_packet_create_data_done` calls
  `wg_timers_any_authenticated_packet_sent` (`send.c:248`), which deletes
  the keepalive timer (`timers.c:168-171`). So two received data messages
  with no transmission between them produce **one** keepalive, not two.
- `wg_expired_new_handshake` (`:91-103`): clear the endpoint source, then
  `..._queued_handshake_initiation(peer, false)` (attempts reset,
  `send.c:58-59`).
- `wg_expired_zero_key_material` (`:105-120`): if `!is_dead`, `wg_peer_get`
  and `queue_work(handshake_send_wq, &clear_peer_work)` (a second queue
  drops the extra reference). The work,
  `wg_queued_expired_zero_key_material` (`:122-133`), runs
  `wg_noise_handshake_clear` (`noise.c:88-96`: index entry removed,
  handshake zeroed) and `wg_noise_keypairs_clear` (`noise.c:151-176`),
  then `wg_peer_put`.
- `wg_expired_send_persistent_keepalive` (`:135-142`): a keepalive (with
  the same `alloc_skb` site) if the interval is still nonzero.

No callback sends on the wire itself: each queues work (the handshake
send worker, the clear work, or through `wg_packet_create_data` the
encrypt worker), or arms and deletes timers. `handshake_send_wq` is
`WQ_UNBOUND` (`device.c:338-339`); the clear work and the send worker
share it, FIFO on one CPU.

### 2.2 Arming and deleting (`kernel/time/timer.c`)

- `mod_timer` (`:1240-1243`) is `__mod_timer(timer, expires, 0)`
  (`:1064-1190`). On a pending timer with the same `expires` it returns
  at once (`:1087`); otherwise it locks the base, `forward_timer_base`
  (`:1018-1021`: `base->clk` catches up to `jiffies`, or to
  `next_expiry` if **any** timer is overdue, `:994-1016`), and computes
  the bucket: same bucket → only `expires` is updated (`:1123-1130`);
  else detach (`:1144`), `timer->expires = expires` (`:1173`), enqueue
  (`:1181-1183`).
- **The wheel rounds up.** `calc_wheel_index` (`:584-631`) picks a level
  by `delta = expires - base->clk` (`LVL_START(n) = 63 << 3(n-1)`:
  63, 504, 4032, 32256, 258048; `:154-171`), and `calc_index` (`:567-580`)
  sets `bucket_expiry = ((expires >> 3n) + 1) << 3n`: a timer fires **no
  earlier than `expires + 1` and no later than `expires + 8^n`** ticks.
  At HZ 250: level 2 is 504–4031 ticks (2.0–16.1 s: the 5 s, 10 s and
  15 s timers; up to 64 ticks, 256 ms, late); level 3 is 4032–32255 ticks
  (16.1–129 s: a 25 s persistent keepalive, `REKEY_AFTER_TIME` 120 s; up
  to 512 ticks, 2 s); level 4 is ≥ 32256 ticks (≥ 129 s: 165 s, 180 s,
  540 s; up to 4096 ticks, 16.4 s). `enqueue_timer` (`:655-690`) records
  the bucket in the timer's flags and lowers `base->next_expiry`.
- `timer_pending` is "hashed in a bucket" (`timer.h:145-148`).
  `del_timer` → `timer_delete` → `__timer_delete(timer, false)`
  (`timer.h:200`, `timer.c:1451`, `:1407-1430`): detach if pending, under
  the base lock. `timer_delete_sync` (`:1721`, `:1638-1680`) also waits for
  a running callback (`:1498-1520`, `base->running_timer`), which cannot
  happen on one CPU (a softirq is not preempted by a task).
- Without `NO_HZ_COMMON` there is one base per CPU (`NR_BASES 1`,
  `:195-204`), so every WireGuard timer is in `timer_bases[0]`.

### 2.3 Expiry: tick → `TIMER_SOFTIRQ` → `call_timer_fn`

1. The periodic tick (`tick_periodic`, `tick-common.c:86-103`) takes
   `jiffies_lock` and `jiffies_seq` (`jiffies.c:43-45`), advances
   `tick_next_period` (`:93`), `do_timer(1)` (`timekeeping.c:2325-2329`:
   `jiffies_64 += ticks`), `update_wall_time()` (`:98`), then
   `update_process_times` (`:101`; `timer.c:2514-2531`) →
   `run_local_timers` (`:2462-2505`): `hrtimer_run_queues` (low-res
   hrtimers expire here, in hardirq, `hrtimer.c:1944`), then
   `raise_softirq(TIMER_SOFTIRQ)` iff `jiffies >= base->next_expiry`
   (`:2502-2504`). The tick device is in **periodic** mode: `TICK_ONESHOT`
   is not built (`kernel/time/Makefile:18`), and `tick_handle_periodic`
   returns before its catch-up loop unless the device is in oneshot state
   (`tick-common.c:120-124`), which only a device without a periodic mode
   gets (`tick_setup_periodic`, `:151-180`); both the LAPIC timer and the
   PIT have one.
2. At interrupt exit, `__irq_exit_rcu` runs pending softirqs unless the
   interrupted context was itself in interrupt or had BH disabled
   (`softirq.c:652-665`, `:444-461`); otherwise they run at the next
   `local_bh_enable` (`:384-410`, the non-RT variant) or in ksoftirqd.
   `handle_softirqs` (`:536-600`) runs the vectors in bit order:
   `TIMER_SOFTIRQ` (bit 1) before `NET_RX_SOFTIRQ` (bit 3)
   (`interrupt.h:557-566`), each `h->action()` at `:579`. Vectors never
   nest: a hardirq during a softirq does not run softirqs
   (`in_interrupt()` at `:661`).
3. `run_timer_softirq` (`timer.c:2447-2460`) → `__run_timer_base`
   (`:2424-2435`) → `__run_timers` (`:2390-2422`): **while
   `jiffies >= base->clk && jiffies >= base->next_expiry`**, collect the
   buckets at `base->clk = next_expiry` (`collect_expired_timers`,
   `:1854-1876`), `base->clk++`, recompute `next_expiry`, and
   `expire_timers` per level (`:1813-1850`). So after a jump in `jiffies`
   every due bucket is run in one pass, in bucket order, and the pass is
   proportional to the number of due buckets, not to the ticks skipped —
   except for timers that re-arm **relative to their old expiry**, which
   run once per missed period in the same pass (the clocksource watchdog,
   `clocksource.c:593`: `expires += WATCHDOG_INTERVAL`, 1080 runs after a
   540 s jump, microseconds each; WireGuard re-arms relative to `jiffies`,
   so each of its timers runs at most once per pass).
4. `expire_timers`: `base->running_timer = timer`, **`detach_timer(timer,
   true)`** (`:1828-1829`: the callback sees its own timer not pending),
   the base lock dropped with irqs enabled (`:1845`), and `call_timer_fn`
   (`:1769-1811`), which calls **`fn(timer)` at `:1795`** between the
   `timer_expire_entry/exit` tracepoints.

### 2.4 Clocks and the clocksource

- **`jiffies`** (`jiffies.h:85-86`) is a plain load. WireGuard uses it
  only for timer expiries (`timers.c:62,87,149,159,185,206,216`). It is
  the one place where a clock reaches kernel data without a reader
  function; the choice-stream strategy left it a declared gap, which
  section 3.3 closes at the timer API.
- **Coarse boot time**, `ktime_get_coarse_boottime_ns`
  (`timekeeping.h:141-144` → `ktime_get_coarse_with_offset(TK_OFFS_BOOT)`,
  `timekeeping.c:804-821`: `tkr_mono.base + offs_boot + xtime_nsec`,
  updated at the tick), is every other WireGuard clock
  (`wg_birthdate_has_expired`, `timers.h:24-29`; `send.c:25,29,39,66,89,
  102,134,365`; `receive.c:115-117,232,253`; `noise.c:396,637,650`;
  `noise.h:103`; `cookie.c:23,96-99,171,225`). Recorded as kind 208.
- `walltime_last_handshake` is real time (`timers.c:197`, kind 205),
  record-only.

Both advance at the tick: `jiffies` by `do_timer`, the timekeeper's
clocks by `update_wall_time` → `timekeeping_advance`
(`timekeeping.c:2188-2265`), which accumulates the **clocksource's**
cycles since the last update (`clocksource_delta`, `:2203-2205`;
`timekeeping_internal.h:18-26`, which only clamps a delta above 0.875 of
the counter's mask, `clocksource.c:986-999`).

**The clocksource on this VM** (the audit's finding, verified):
- without `ACPI` there is no HPET and no PM timer, so the candidates are
  the TSC (rating 300) and `refined-jiffies` (rating 2, `jiffies.c:77-84`,
  registered by `arch/x86/kernel/setup.c:1188`), whose `read` is `jiffies`
  itself (`jiffies.c:16`);
- `qemu64` has no `CONSTANT_TSC`, so the TSC keeps
  `CLOCK_SOURCE_MUST_VERIFY` (`tsc.c:1190-1200`; the watchdog is disabled
  only for constant, nonstop TSCs with `TSC_ADJUST`, or by
  `tsc=reliable`/`tsc=nowatchdog`, `:1553-1561`, `:321-331`), and
  `refined-jiffies`, the only clocksource without `MUST_VERIFY`, becomes
  its **watchdog** (`clocksource_select_watchdog`, `clocksource.c:638-668`);
- the watchdog runs every 0.5 s (`WATCHDOG_INTERVAL`, `:111`) and marks
  the TSC unstable when the TSC and `jiffies` deltas differ by more than
  `32 ms + 31.25 ms` (the TSC's margin, `tsc.c:1168`, plus
  `WATCHDOG_THRESHOLD` for a clocksource registered without a frequency,
  `clocksource.c:1194,117`; the check at `:514-516`), unless the interval
  itself exceeded 1 s, when the check is skipped
  (`WATCHDOG_INTERVAL_MAX_NS`, `:112,502-510`). `loglevel=3` hides the
  warning. After the demotion the timekeeper runs on `refined-jiffies`
  for the rest of the boot.

So the current oracle's timekeeper is the TSC until a stall of more than
63 ms within a half-second interval demotes it, which host load on the
TCG VM can cause at any time. Nothing today depends on which it is; D3's
time control does (section 4).

## 3. Capture

### 3.1 Timer dispatch sections at `call_timer_fn`

A kernel change in `kernel/time/timer.c` (unanchored; `correspondence/
kcov.json` anchors `drivers/net/wireguard/*` and the FS files, not
`kernel/time/*`): in `call_timer_fn`,

```c
	struct kcov_dispatch_section section;	/* LEANUX */
	...
	trace_timer_expire_entry(timer, baseclk);
	kcov_dispatch_start(&section, fn);
	fn(timer);
	kcov_dispatch_stop(&section);
	trace_timer_expire_exit(timer);
```

exactly as `process_one_work` and the NET_RX vector are sectioned today
(`gen-choice-stream.py:662-698`). A section opens iff `fn` is in the
**armed dispatch set**, which gains the five `wg_expired_*` callbacks and
`wg_queued_expired_zero_key_material` (`harness/linux.py:37-39`,
`DISPATCH_SYMBOLS`; 12 of `DISPATCH_MAX` 16). The start record (kind 300)
carries `fn`'s address, resolved by the host to its symbol as for workers
(`evaluator/check.py:292-299`). Every other timer that the jump fires
(vmstat, `addrconf_verify`, neighbour GC, the clocksource watchdog, RCU)
opens no section and records nothing: the armed-set policy of the
strategy's section 4.4, unchanged.

**Nesting, resolved.** The strategy excluded `TIMER_SOFTIRQ` from the
vector section because a timer section inside it would nest
(`kcov.c:880`, the softirq double-start check). D3 sections the
**callback, not the vector**:
- two timer callbacks are sequential `call_timer_fn` calls, never nested;
- `TIMER_SOFTIRQ` and `NET_RX_SOFTIRQ` run one after the other in
  `handle_softirqs` (section 2.3, item 2), so a timer section and a NET_RX
  section are siblings. A callback that raises NET_RX (none of WireGuard's
  does; they queue work) would still not nest, because the vector runs
  after the timer vector returns;
- a tick that lands inside a **task** section (a worker, or the trace
  process's own syscall) gives a softirq section nested in it, which KCOV
  supports (`kcov_remote_start`, `kcov.c:872-884,926-929`: the task's mode
  is saved and the per-CPU softirq area used) and the D1 machinery already
  consumes for NET_RX (`Env.sectionOf`, `skipAmbient`). Section 5.3 says
  where the mirror accepts such a section and where it is a fail-noisy
  mismatch;
- a tick in the idle task (the trace process asleep in its wait) gives a
  top-level softirq section, as ksoftirqd's NET_RX does in D2's order (a).
  Idle-task softirq sections are moved to the remote area like any other
  (`kcov.c:848-932`, `:1020-1050`); the audit confirmed it (Q3).

`kcov_dispatch_start`'s own guard (`gen-choice-stream.py:468`) still
drops, counted, any section that would nest in a softirq section.

### 3.2 Records for firing

A fired timer is captured by its section: start (kind 300, `fn`), the
callback's choices (the retransmit's clock read at `send.c:66`, the
keepalive's at `send.c:365`, the jitter draw of a re-arm) and stop (kind
301). Which timers fired, and in what order, is therefore **captured**, as
the strategy wants for dispatch choices: nothing is predicted from
`jiffies` arithmetic alone.

### 3.3 The arm record (kind 303) [AGENT; adopted, USER 2026-10-05]

**Decision [USER 2026-10-05]:** include the arm record ("re armed timer - agree"); the envelope-only fallback is not taken.

`jiffies` reaches data at `mod_timer`. A hook at `mod_timer`'s entry
(`timer.c:1240`) writes one record per call for timers whose function is
in the armed set: `kcov_choice_ip(303, _RET_IP_, {jiffies, expires}, 16)`.
Its consumer frame is `timers.c:37` (`mod_peer_timer`, inlined into its
seven callers: one site for all, like `timers.h:28`). Every WireGuard arm
happens in a recorded context (workers, NET_RX sections, timer sections;
none in a syscall), so the record count is exact.

**The hook is lock-free** (audit M5): `mod_timer` is hot across the
kernel, so the membership test is a `READ_ONCE` snapshot of
`kcov_dispatch_kcov` and the `funcs` array, no `kcov_remote_lock`. The
set changes only while no trace runs (`kcov_dispatch_load` before the
fork; disarm in `kcov_remote_reset`), and `kcov_choice_ip` already gates
on the recording context, so outside a trace the hook costs one load.

**The check is tick-tolerant** (audit M3): the caller computes
`jiffies + delay` before `mod_timer` reads `jiffies`, and at two sites a
recorded jitter draw sits between (`timers.c:148-150,184-186`); a tick
can land in between. The mirror accepts `expires − jiffies ∈ {delay − 1,
delay}`; a larger gap is a hidden mismatch (a stall the run must not
hide).

What it buys, each with evidence in section 8:
- the mirror's **expiry data** becomes checkable: today a wrong delay in
  the mirror is invisible (pending flags only): mutant D3-M8;
- the known survivor **C-M3** (no traversal re-arm after receiving)
  becomes rejected: the re-arm's record is missing;
- the keepalive callback's **re-arm** (section 2.1), which the tx worker
  deletes before it can fire, is visible only through its arm record:
  mutant D3-M2;
- the environment knows **elapsed `jiffies`** exactly at every arm, so the
  firing window (section 5.3) does not rest on the trace's real-time
  bound, and natural firings within the bound are in the domain.

### 3.4 The advance record (kind 304)

The time-advance operation (section 4.3) writes one record in the trace
process's task stream: `{jiffies before, jiffies after}`, resolved by kind
(like 300–302), consumed by `Env.advance`. It gives the environment the
exact `jiffies` after the jump.

### 4.2 The semantic rule for the virtual advance

The steering rule (steering design, section 3): a supplied value must be a
possible output of its source, and must not interact with an unsteered
hidden input. For a scheduling or time choice: the resulting execution
must be one Linux can produce. After `advance(N)` (C-jiffies):
- `jiffies` has moved by N; at the next tick the timekeeper moves by N + 1
  ticks as it does for any jiffies clocksource whose counter jumped (a
  stalled VM); the wheel's catch-up pass runs the due buckets in bucket
  order (`timer.c:2400-2420`); pending low-res hrtimers expire at that tick
  (`hrtimer.c:1944`), as they do when time passes;
- no WireGuard code runs between the old and the new values: the advance
  is a trace operation issued at an idle point (the trace process in a
  syscall, workers idle, no softirq pending), and the timer softirq that
  the tick raises runs after `update_wall_time` in the same tick
  (`tick-common.c:95-101`), so every callback sees the advanced clocks;
- the recorded clock values the mirror consumes are Linux's actual values.

Differences from N real seconds, each declared, none touching modeled
state:
1. the N·HZ intermediate ticks' side effects do not happen
   (`sched_tick`, `rcu_sched_clock_irq` (Tiny RCU here),
   `calc_global_load`, `account_process_tick`, `profile_tick`);
2. `sched_clock`/`local_clock` (the scheduler's clock, printk timestamps)
   are not moved by the advance; what they do afterwards depends on
   whether the TSC has been marked unstable (then `sched_clock` is bounded
   to the tick's GTOD); nothing mirrored reads them;
3. the clocks and `jiffies` move in one step rather than per tick;
4. **every clock read is tick-granular (4 ms)** on a jiffies clocksource,
   for every trace, not only D3's. That is a possible Linux execution (a
   machine without a usable TSC), and no check depends on sub-tick
   resolution: the flood test compares coarse time (`noise.c:636-637`),
   the steerable `tai64n_now` domain is unchanged, `walltime_last_handshake`
   is record-only, and the mirror consumes whatever was recorded. The
   guest's own `clock_gettime` becomes a syscall (no vDSO clock mode for
   jiffies); its records land after the operation's count and are ignored
   (`guest.c:629-632,718`).

A real-wait control (section 8) shows the same decisions and record kinds
as the virtual trace. Option D is kept as a later, separately justified
control for the suspend case (`wg_pm_notification` is out of scope).

### 4.3 Recommendation [AGENT]: C-jiffies — **a decision for the user**

Both C variants change the oracle's boot parameters (`-append` in
`harness/linux.py:466`), so the choice is the user's. Weighed:

| | C-tsc (+ `tsc=reliable` or `tsc=nowatchdog`) | C-jiffies (`clocksource=refined-jiffies`) |
|---|---|---|
| Kernel code | `timekeeping_forward_now`, `tk_xtime_add`, `timekeeping_update(TK_MIRROR \| TK_CLOCK_WAS_SET)` (no `TK_CLEAR_NTP`; no `clock_was_set`, which only reaches timerfd here, `hrtimer.c:951-958`), then the `jiffies` half; fail closed unless `tkr_mono.clock` is the TSC | the `jiffies` half only; fail closed unless `tkr_mono.clock` is `refined-jiffies` |
| Correctness dependence | on the clocksource type: a demotion would double the advance; prevented by the boot parameter and caught by the fail-closed check and the batch clocksource check | none beyond the pinned clocksource, which the same checks enforce |
| Clock resolution | sub-tick for fine reads, as today | tick-granular for all reads, for every trace |
| `MONOTONIC_RAW` | left behind (declared) | moves too |
| Watchdog | disabled: `tsc=reliable` declares a TCG TSC reliable, which a host stall contradicts silently (today the watchdog would at least demote) | the TSC is still watched and will be demoted by the first advance of 16–125 ticks; irrelevant to the pinned timekeeper, visible only in `sched_clock` behaviour (4.2, item 2). `tsc=nowatchdog` can be added if that proves noisy |
| Determinism of today's oracle | fixes the latent mid-batch demotion by forbidding it | fixes it by pinning the fallback |

**Recommendation: C-jiffies.** It is a fraction of the code, has no
dependence on the clocksource's type, moves every clock by construction,
and turns today's latent nondeterminism (a watchdog demotion under host
load changing the clock resolution mid-batch) into a pinned, checked
property. Its one cost, tick-granular clocks, is a possible Linux
execution that no current check distinguishes. If the user prefers
sub-tick resolution, C-tsc with its fixes is the alternative.

**The operation.** `wg_advance {ticks}`: the guest calls `ioctl(3,
KCOV_LEANUX_TIME_ADVANCE, &ticks)` on its own KCOV descriptor (fd 3; the
ioctl needs no instance state, and the LEANUX cases sit before
`kcov_ioctl`'s locked path, `kcov.c:695-760`, as the steering ioctls do),
then waits `ADVANCE_WAIT_US` (100 ms, as `QUEUE_WAIT_US`; stream K
measures). Within the wait the next tick advances the timekeeper, raises
`TIMER_SOFTIRQ`, the due callbacks run in sections, the work they queue
runs in sections, and loopback delivers. The remote records of all of it
land in this operation (the guest reads `remote[0]` after the wait,
`guest.c:753-758`).

The kernel helper, `leanux_time_advance(ticks)` in `timekeeping.c` (which
includes `tick-internal.h`, `:28`, for `jiffies_lock`, `jiffies_seq` and
`tick_next_period`, `tick-internal.h:22`):
1. under `timekeeper_lock`, return `-EOPNOTSUPP` unless
   `tk_core.timekeeper.tkr_mono.clock->name` is `"refined-jiffies"`
   (fail closed: on any other timekeeper the advance would be
   inconsistent);
2. `raw_spin_lock_irqsave(&jiffies_lock)`, `write_seqcount_begin(&
   jiffies_seq)`, `tick_next_period += ticks * TICK_NSEC` (cosmetic in
   periodic mode, kept for symmetry with `tick_periodic`), `do_timer(ticks)`,
   unlock;
3. the ioctl handler in `kcov.c` records kind 304 `{before, after}` and
   returns 0. It does not raise `TIMER_SOFTIRQ`: the next tick does
   (`timer.c:2502-2504`), after `update_wall_time`, as when time passes.

Steps of at most 2^31 ticks; the generator uses what the trace needs
(1450 ticks for a retransmit, 139200 for the zero-key timer). The jiffies
clocksource's `max_raw_delta` is 0.875 × 2^32 ticks (`clocksource.c:999`,
`jiffies.c:37`), far beyond any step, and `timekeeping_advance`'s
logarithmic accumulation handles the offset (`:2218-2230`).

**Guest and harness.**
- The trace bound (`guest.c:51-53,733-738`, `REKEY_BOUND_NS`, read from
  `CLOCK_BOOTTIME`) subtracts the virtual advance: the guest keeps
  `advanced_ns` and compares real elapsed time. The wait is a `usleep`
  after the ioctl, so no sleep of the trace process is in flight when
  time jumps.
- **Batch clocksource check** (audit C1): the guest reads
  `/sys/devices/system/clocksource/clocksource0/current_clocksource` and
  `available_clocksource` at batch start and end and prints a
  `LEANUX_CLOCKSOURCE` row; the harness fails the batch unless both are
  the expected name, which is recorded with the run's provenance next to
  the QEMU command (`harness/linux.py:468`) and named in the kernel
  identity file's header (`kernel/identity-x86_64-net-choice.txt`), since
  the oracle is now "this build booted with these parameters".
- `rcu_cpu_stall_suppress` is not needed: Tiny RCU has no stall detector;
  the hung-task and lockup detectors are not configured.

**Why keep the 4 s bound.** It bounds real elapsed time, which the firing
window (5.3) uses where no `jiffies` is recorded, and it keeps the number
of traces per batch high on the TCG VM. With the arm record it is no
longer needed for correctness, only for throughput.

### 4.4 Fail-noisiness of the supply

- A section for a timer the mirror has as not pending, or not yet due:
  a hidden mismatch (the record is at a site the mirror does not expect).
- A target timer that did not fire: the trace's `expect` label is not
  reached (`check.py:778-779`, "expected outcome not reached").
- The helper refuses any timekeeper but the pinned one (`-EOPNOTSUPP`),
  and the batch fails if the clocksource is not the expected one at start
  or end.
- `jiffies` recorded by consecutive records must advance by at least the
  advances between them and by at most that plus the real-time bound: a
  tick handler that re-counted the jump would fail here.
- The dropped- and opened-section counters and `section_problems` apply
  to timer sections unchanged.

### 4.5 Open questions, with the audit's answers

- **Q1, answered:** the tick device is periodic (`TICK_ONESHOT` unset,
  `kernel/time/Makefile:18`; `tick-common.c:120-124`). `tick_next_period`
  is cosmetic. Stream K still confirms it once from `/proc/timer_list`.
- **Q2, answered:** `clock_was_set` has no effect here (`hrtimer.c:951-958`
  → timerfd only); C-jiffies does not call it.
- **Q3, answered:** idle-task softirq sections are moved to the remote
  area (`kcov.c:848-932`, `:1020-1050`).
- **Q4 (new):** whether `clocksource=refined-jiffies` on the current
  oracle changes any recorded value a check compares exactly. Expected
  none (4.2, item 4); stream K runs the existing suites on the new boot
  parameters before anything else, as the measurement.
- **Q5 (new):** whether the TSC's demotion under the pinned timekeeper
  (4.3) produces console noise or `sched_clock` effects worth
  `tsc=nowatchdog`. Decided by the same run.

### 5.3 The environment: fired timers from records [AGENT, simplified after the audit]

`Env.advance ticks` (the `wg_advance` operation, after `fdget 3` as every
fixture call does, `Syscalls.lean:50-60`):
1. consume the kind-304 record `(J₀, J₁)`; `J₁ − J₀ == ticks` else a
   hidden mismatch; remember `J₁` as the last known `jiffies`;
2. **timer sections**, while the next record starts a timer section for
   `fn`: the peer is the one whose timer of kind `fn` **is pending and
   could be due** (below); exactly one, else `unsupported` (several peers'
   timers of one kind in one pass: their order follows the arming order,
   which the mirror could compute but does not need yet). Clear the
   field, `runWorker (.timerX) (expiredX pid)`;
3. **the work they queued**, as today: `runSendWorkers` (the send worker
   and now the clear worker, FIFO on `handshake_send_wq`, ordered by
   records), `runCryptWorkers`, `deliverSent`.

**Could be due.** A timer with recorded `expires = e` fires no earlier than
`e + 1` (section 2.2), by a softirq whose `jiffies` is at most `J_hi`:
after an advance `J_hi = J₁ + W` (`W` the wait in ticks, 25); at any other
idle point `J_hi = J_last + R + W` (`R` the real-time bound, 1000 ticks;
`J_last` the last recorded `jiffies`). A section is accepted iff the
timer is pending and `e < J_hi`. That is the whole rule.

What the first draft had beyond it — the wheel's level granularity, a
"lag" term for arms made while the base was behind, a must-fire check and
an order check — is dropped (audit M4): with `e` and `J₁` exact and the
firings captured by sections, it caught nothing the records and the
`expect` labels do not, and its lag rule was unsound (`__forward_timer_base`
leaves `base->clk` behind whenever **any** timer is overdue,
`timer.c:994-1016`, not only inside a callback). "This timer must fire" is
a per-trace expectation: the generator chooses the advance with margin
(section 7) and the trace's `expect` labels assert the firing; a lateness
the margin did not cover fails noisily there and the trace is fixed.

**Idle points.** Timer sections are accepted at the places the guest
sleeps: inside `wg_advance`, and in the waits after `wg_inject`,
`wg_xmit`, `wg_set`, `wg_up` and before `wg_observe` (`Env.inject`,
`afterXmit`, `runSendWorkers`, `observe`), where a natural firing of a
short timer (a persistent keepalive of 1–4 s) lands. A timer section
anywhere else, nested at an arbitrary point of a worker's body or a
syscall, is a hidden mismatch: the mirror cannot run a callback mid-body,
and says so. The waits are where the kernel is idle, so this is the
common case by construction.

## 8. Evidence: what pays for itself

Each item names what no existing check catches. A mutant that turns a
trace `unsupported` counts as rejected: `unsupported_path` is reported as
the trace's problem (`check.py:138`), the run's status is `fail`, and
`wg-mutants.py` requires exactly that (`scripts/wg-mutants.py:8`).

**Mutants** (mirror, rejected only on D3 traces):
- D3-M1: the retransmit does not increment `attempts` (or `>` becomes
  `≥`): `d3_retransmit_gives_up` gives up a cycle early or late
  (`tx_dropped`, sections).
- D3-M3: firing does not clear the timer: the next `data_received`'s
  pending test differs, so an arm record is consumed or not.
- D3-M4: the clear worker clears keypairs but not the handshake: the
  index removal's control flow (A2) and the next initiation's state.
- D3-M5: send-side `keep_key_fresh` at 180 s instead of 120 s: no
  initiation at 121 s (behavior, A2).
- D3-M6: receive-side `keep_key_fresh` does not set
  `sent_lastminute_handshake`: an extra clock record on the second inject
  (hidden mismatch).
- D3-M7: `send_staged_packets` ignores `REJECT_AFTER_TIME`: a data message
  instead of an initiation (bytes).

**The arm record's evidence** (section 3.3):
- **D3-M8**: `timer_new_handshake` armed at `KEEPALIVE_TIMEOUT*HZ` instead
  of `(KEEPALIVE_TIMEOUT + REKEY_TIMEOUT)*HZ` — invisible today, rejected
  only by the kind-303 `expires` check.
- **D3-M2**: the keepalive callback ignores `need_another_keepalive`: the
  re-arm never fires (section 2.1), so its only trace is the arm record
  inside the timer section, which the mutant leaves unconsumed (hidden
  mismatch) in `d3_keepalive_rearm_deleted`.
- **C-M3**, the known survivor (no traversal re-arm after receiving):
  rejected by a missing arm record on the receive path.

**Controls:**
- `d3_persistent_natural` vs `d3_persistent_keepalive`: a real-time
  firing and a virtual one show the same decision sequence and record
  kinds, differing only in clock values (the semantic argument of 4.2,
  witnessed).
- `d3_keepalive_rearm_deleted`: one keepalive, not two.
- An advance with wait 0 (a guest variant): the sections land in the
  next operation and the mirror meets an unexpected section there.
- The `jiffies`-progress check and the batch clocksource check: not
  exercised by a built variant; they are the guards for Q1 and C1.

**Not claimed:** the opened-section count has no new mutant; the clear
worker's `peer_put` is covered by the existing refcount decisions.

## 10. Risks

- **Closed by the audit: the double advance.** A `tk_xtime_add` plus
  `do_timer` helper on a jiffies timekeeper advances the clocks twice;
  this VM's TSC is watched by jiffies and would have been demoted by the
  first 16–125-tick advance (section 2.4). C-jiffies has no second half
  to double, the helper refuses any other timekeeper, and the batch check
  catches a clocksource change.
- **Medium: other kernel timers the jump fires.** They run unsectioned
  (vmstat, `addrconf_verify`, neighbour GC, the clocksource watchdog,
  writeback). None touches wg0's state: wg0's addresses are static with
  infinite lifetimes (`guest.c:396-432`, `IFA_F_NODAD`), its link-local
  generation is `NONE` (`device.c:48`). Fail-noisy if wrong: a cut or
  hidden mismatch. The first draft's claim that the clocksource watchdog
  is unaffected was false (it compares the TSC against `jiffies`); under
  C-jiffies the demotion it causes does not touch the timekeeper.
- **Low: the catch-up pass's cost after 540 s.** Proportional to due
  buckets, plus one run per missed period for timers that re-arm relative
  to their old expiry (the watchdog's 1080 runs, microseconds each; it
  stops once the TSC is demoted).
- **Low: the attempt-exhaustion trace's budget.** 20 waits of 0.1 s; if
  tight, the class is a stretch target.
- **Low: the mirror's timer representation change** touches D1/D2 code;
  the existing suites are the regression.
- **Sequencing (audit M6):** every existing WireGuard trace arms timers,
  so once the mirror consumes arm records the D1 and D2 corpora, the
  generated suites, the mutant baselines and the steering controls all
  need the D3 kernel and its boot parameters. The whole corpus is re-run
  on it before signoff (section 11).

### 13.3 The D3 kernel (steps 2 and 3)

- **Kernel.** In `call_timer_fn`: `kcov_dispatch_start/stop` around
  `fn(timer)`. In `mod_timer`:

  lock-free `READ_ONCE` membership test that writes kind 303 through
  `kcov_choice_ip`. `leanux_time_advance` in `timekeeping.c` holds
  `timekeeper_lock`, fails closed unless the clocksource name is
  `refined-jiffies`, then under `jiffies_lock`/`jiffies_seq` moves
  `tick_next_period` and calls `do_timer(ticks)`.

  the locked path in `kcov_ioctl`. It rejects `ticks` outside
  [1, 2^31] (`-EINVAL`) and a caller that is not collecting (`-EPERM`), and
  writes kind 304 `{before, after}` with `kcov_choice_write`.
- **The arm record is gated off by default** [AGENT]. With it on, 64 of
  the 96 WireGuard corpus traces fail on the D3 kernel. All 618 problems
  are "choice kind 303: choice from an undeclared consumer" at
  `timers.c:37` (`.artifacts/check/d3-arm-on-wireguard`). The switch is a
  load-time flag: the dispatch-load argument's second word
  (`{u32 n; u32 flags; u64 funcs[n]}`), bit 0 `KCOV_DISPATCH_ARM_RECORDS`,
  set from the wire's new `A 0|1` line, which comes from
  `harness/linux.py` `ARM_RECORDS = False` (`run_linux(...,
  arm_records=)`). The mirror stream turns it on when it consumes 303.
- **Harness and guest.** `DISPATCH_SYMBOLS` has 12 entries; the evaluator
  requires section sites ⊆ `DISPATCH_SYMBOLS` (`check.py` `run_chunk`),
  not equality. Opcode 14 is `wg_advance {ticks}` (`harness/trace.py`).
  The guest reads `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` before the coverage window, calls the
  ioctl on fd 3 (a failure fails the trace), then waits.
  - **The wait has two parts.** A sleep begun right after the ioctl would
    end at the very next tick, since monotonic time jumps there. So the
    guest first polls (1 ms sleeps, at most about 1 s) until the monotonic
    clock has absorbed `ticks × 4 ms`, then sleeps `ADVANCE_WAIT_US`
    (100 ms).
  - The trace bound subtracts `ticks × 4 ms`. A refined-jiffies tick is
    about 4.0002 ms, so the bound is about 26 ms stricter after 139200
    ticks.
  - The guest prints `LEANUX_CLOCKSOURCE` rows (current, available) at
    batch start and end; the harness fails the batch unless both are
    `refined-jiffies`. `-append` comes from `BOOT_PARAMETERS`, and
    provenance records the expected clocksource and the arm-record switch.
- **Existing suites on the D3 kernel** (arm records off, timer sections
  armed): **1369/1369 agreement, 39260 operations, 323087 operand pairs**

- **Probes** (`scripts/d3-k-probes.py`, Linux only, arm records on;

  advance, as a top-level section in the remote stream: the tick lands
  while the trace process sleeps, and no timer section nests in another
  section.
  - `k_retransmit` (2 × 1450): `wg_expired_retransmit_handshake`, then the
    send worker, NET_RX, a new arm (d = 1331, 1316), and an initiation at
    the listener each time.
  - `k_keepalive` (2600): `wg_expired_send_keepalive` with its +10 s re-arm
    record inside the timer section (d = 2500, caller
    `wg_expired_send_keepalive`), then the encrypt and tx workers and a

  - `k_new_handshake` (3950): `wg_expired_new_handshake`, then an
    initiation.
  - `k_persistent` (6800): `wg_expired_send_persistent_keepalive`, then a

  - Every advance's records (the timer sections, the workers they queue,
