# W5 + W6: receive shapes and the stack's reactions to inner packets — design, 2026-10-06

Status: **proposed** [AGENT], for the user's decisions in section 12. The
direction is the user's [USER 2026-10-06]: 100% of reasonable single-CPU
WireGuard; everything in reach that can be differentially tested gets built.
This note covers the census rows W5 (receive shapes) and W6 (inner-packet
stack reactions) of [the census](2026-10-06-wireguard-census.md). P8–P10
stay out.

The base is the integrated D3+L+A+E tip. Mirror and harness references are
to `task/d3-int` unless named otherwise. Kernel line numbers are in the
unpatched `.deps/linux` (6.12.110); the fixture facts are those of
`harness/guest.c` (`route_into_wg0`: an address and a default route via wg0
per family; two UDP listeners at 51999; the dual-stack sender `SEND_FD` at
`[::]:51998` with `IP_PMTUDISC_DO` and `IPV6_AUTOFLOWLABEL 0`; no TCP
sockets; `forwarding`, `rp_filter`, `accept_local`, `accept_source_route`,
`route_localnet` at their defaults, 0).

## 0. Summary

- **W5** moves four receive-side shapes from `unsupported` to modeled, all
  of them inside the delivery cut that D2 placed at `napi_complete_done`:
  the outer DS field with the ECN decapsulation's `set_ce`/`set_ect1` inner
  rewrites; the small silent drops and the one trim of the IPv4/IPv6 input
  predicate; a scoped (link-local) outer source giving `sin6_scope_id`; the
  device tstats. Each is observed: the inner TOS reaches the sender's
  `recvmsg` as a cmsg, the outer `ds` of wg0's own datagrams reaches the
  listeners the same way, the scope reaches the dump, the counters reach
  `IFLA_STATS64`.
- **W6** adds the stack's reactions, modeled in the environment: the reply
  bytes are computed exactly (they are AEAD-encrypted, so nothing less
  works) and handed to the mirror's `wg_xmit` at the cut, inside the NAPI
  poll's NET_RX section; the existing encrypt/tx/loopback machinery and
  section records order the rest. Shapes: TCP RST (IPv4 verified **DF, id
  0**; IPv6), ICMPv6 errors (port unreachable, unknown next header, unknown
  option, fragment time exceeded), ICMPv4 errors (the same four, gated on
  one capture), two-fragment reassembly with `ip_expire`/`ip6_frag_expire`
  as environment timer units under the D3 time advance, IPv4 options and
  IPv6 extension headers.
- **Needs.** No kernel change for W5, and none for W6 except the ICMPv4
  class: the IPv4 identifier of a non-DF reply is drawn from a boot-random
  counter and must be captured (section 3.3). Fixture changes: a per-op
  sender TOS, `recvmsg` with cmsg, a link-local address on `lo` with a second
  sender socket, two sysctls (`net.ipv6.auto_flowlabels=0`; the ICMP rate
  limits at 0, section 2.4.4), `DISPATCH_SYMBOLS` + 2 (14 of 16).
- **Decisions for the user** (section 12): the identifier capture (a kernel
  change); the two sysctls (oracle configuration); `ip_error` deferred
  (section 2.4.7); whether the tstats instrument pays (section 2.6).

## 1. Target and the governing rule

The stack between `napi_gro_receive` and the sender's socket is a cut: it
is not WireGuard, and the mirror holds only WireGuard's data and control
flow. D2 made the cut **fail-closed by an exact predicate** (the environment
delivers exactly the datagrams Linux delivers, and says `unsupported` for
everything else). W5 and W6 widen that predicate without changing its
nature: every inner packet the generator produces gets a **named outcome**
in the environment — delivered (with its bytes and TOS), dropped (with the
Linux arm that drops it), answered (with the reply's exact bytes), or
`unsupported` (with the reason) — and every outcome is observed, or the
shape is not added.

What the mirror gains is only what WireGuard's own code does with these
packets: the ECN rewrite arms of `INET_ECN_decapsulate` (inlined into
`wg_packet_rx_poll`), the `ds` it computes on transmit, the scope id of an
endpoint, the device counter, and `wg_xmit` entered from softirq context
with a packet the stack built.

## 2. What Linux does with an inner packet (verified)

### 2.1 The outer DS field and ECN decapsulation (W5)

- `wg_packet_receive` stores the outer DS field before queueing a data
  message: `PACKET_CB(skb)->ds = ip_tunnel_get_dsfield(ip_hdr(skb), skb)`
  (receive.c:575; ip_tunnels.h:555-566: the IPv4 `tos`, or the IPv6
  traffic class). Today the fixture's outer TOS is 0 (`Env.senderSource`,
  `tos := 0`), so every ECN arm has a constant outcome.
- `wg_packet_consume_data_done` calls `INET_ECN_decapsulate(skb, ds,
  inner)` for IPv4 (receive.c:389, inner = `ip_hdr(skb)->tos`) and IPv6
  (:393, `ipv6_get_dsfield`) and **ignores the return value**: a "drop"
  verdict (rc 2) still delivers. The arms (inet_ecn.h:251-284, the RFC 6040
  table at :234-243):
  - inner not-ECT: outer not-ECT → nothing; outer ECT(0)/ECT(1)/CE →
    rc 1/2, **no rewrite**;
  - inner ECT or CE, outer CE → `INET_ECN_set_ce` (:172-189): IPv4
    `IP_ECN_set_ce` (:77-103): `ecn = (tos + 1) & 3`; ECT(1) → `check +=
    htons(0xFFFD)`, ECT(0) → `check += htons(0xFFFE)` (ones'-complement
    16-bit adds), then `tos |= CE`; CE stays; IPv6 `IP6_ECN_set_ce`
    (:134-148): the first 32-bit word `|= htonl(CE << 20)`;
  - inner ECT or CE, outer ECT(1) → `INET_ECN_set_ect1` (:208-225): IPv4
    `IP_ECN_set_ect1` (:105-113): only ECT(0) changes, `check += htons(1)`,
    `tos ^= 3`; IPv6 `IP6_ECN_set_ect1` (:150-164): only ECT(0), the word
    `^= htonl(3 << 20)`;
  - all other combinations: nothing.
  The IPv4 checksum adjustments keep a correct header checksum correct and
  a wrong one wrong, so the input predicate (2.2) is evaluated on the
  rewritten bytes.
- **The sender's TOS is one knob for both directions.** `IP_TOS` on the
  dual-stack sender sets `inet->tos` with the ECN bits kept for a datagram
  socket (`__ip_sock_set_tos`, ip_sockglue.c:586-599; the `SOL_IP` level on
  an IPv6 socket goes to `udp_prot.setsockopt`, ipv6_sockglue.c:987) and
  `IPV6_TCLASS` sets `np->tclass` (ipv6_sockglue.c:734-751, ECN kept for
  datagrams). `__ip_make_skb` writes `iph->tos = inet->tos` (ip_output.c:
  1460). So a per-op TOS on the sender gives (a) the outer DS of an
  injected message and (b) the inner TOS of a transmitted packet, whose
  `ds = ip_tunnel_ecn_encap(0, ip_hdr(skb), skb)` (send.c:378;
  `INET_ECN_encapsulate`, inet_ecn.h:46-52: the inner ECN bits, CE → ECT(0))
  becomes the outer TOS/traffic class of wg0's datagram
  (`udp_tunnel_xmit_skb(..., ds, ...)` socket.c:85; `udp_tunnel6_xmit_skb`
  :152). D2 dropped the `ds` mutant because the TOS was 0 and the outer DS
  unobserved; W5 restores it.
- **Observation.** `IP_RECVTOS` yields an `IP_TOS` cmsg of
  `ip_hdr(skb)->tos` (ip_sockglue.c:69-74, :194-197); `IPV6_RECVTCLASS`
  yields `ipv6_get_dsfield(ipv6_hdr(skb))` (datagram.c:644-646). The
  dual-stack sender's `udpv6_recvmsg` delivers either, by the packet's
  family (ipv6/udp.c:484-490). The guest's `recvfrom` becomes `recvmsg`
  on all sockets; every observed datagram gains its TOS.

### 2.4 Reactions (W6)

Every reply below leaves through wg0 (the fixture's default route per
family), so it enters `wg_xmit` **from softirq context, inside the NAPI
poll's NET_RX section** (`napi_complete_done` → `gro_normal_list` → the
IP receive path → the reply's `ip_local_out` → `dev_queue_xmit`). From
there it is D2's path: `wg_packet_send_staged_packets`, the encrypt worker
(a `process_one_work` section after the softirq, or inside the decrypt
worker's `cond_resched` in order (a)), the tx worker, loopback to the
peer's endpoint — the sender, after roaming — whose inbox holds a
`message_data`. The reply's `wg_timers_data_sent` arm is a kind-303 record
inside the NET_RX section, as D3 expects for any recorded context.

#### 2.4.1 TCP RST

A segment to any port of wg0's address finds no socket (the fixture has
none). IPv4: `tcp_v4_rcv` requires `doff ≥ 5` and a pullable header
(tcp_ipv4.c:2209-2231; `skb_checksum_init` is satisfied by
`CHECKSUM_UNNECESSARY`), then `no_tcp_socket` (:2371-2386; the checksum
completion is skipped) → `tcp_v4_send_reset(NULL, skb, …)` (:737-920):
- no reply to a segment with RST (:760-761); the route must be local
  (:766-767);
- `dest/source` swapped, `doff 5`, `rst 1`; with ACK: `seq = ack_seq`;
  without: `ack 1`, `ack_seq = seq + syn + fin + payload length`
  (:770-782); no MD5/AO/MPTCP options in this config;
- `arg.tos = ip_hdr(skb)->tos` (:889); `ip_send_unicast_reply`
  (ip_output.c:1603-1677): the inner IP options are echoed (:1618-1630;
  W6 keeps TCP segments without options), `inet_sk(sk)->tos = tos &
  ~INET_ECN_MASK` (:1649), the TCP checksum is folded in software and
  **`ip_summed = CHECKSUM_NONE`** (:1664-1668);
- the per-CPU control socket has `IP_PMTUDISC_DO` ("Please enforce IP_DF
  and IPID==0 for RST", tcp_ipv4.c:3797-3800): `__ip_make_skb` sets DF
  (ip_output.c:1443-1445), `ignore_df = ip_sk_ignore_df(sk)` is 0 (:1435;
  ip.h:462-467), the socket is unconnected, so `ip_select_ident_segs` gives
  **id 0** (ip.h:559-589, :583-584); TTL `ip_select_ttl` = 64 (:1455);
  tos as above (:1460). **Verified: DF, id 0.**

IPv6: `tcp_v6_rcv` → `no_tcp_socket` (tcp_ipv6.c:1908-1923) →
`tcp_v6_send_reset(NULL, …)` (:1007-1140): no reply to RST (:1030-1031);
unicast destination (:1036-1037); `label = 0` unless `flowlabel_reflect`
has `TCP_RESET` (:1126-1128, default 0); `tcp_v6_send_response(…, rst 1,
tclass = ipv6_get_dsfield(ipv6h), label, …)` (:1133-1135; :860-1000):
window 0, `ack = !th->ack` (:908-910), **`ip_summed = CHECKSUM_PARTIAL`**
with `__tcp_v6_send_check` (:955-957), `ip6_xmit(…, tclass & ~INET_ECN_MASK,
…)` (:996-997): hop limit `ip6_dst_hoplimit` = 64 (ip6_output.c:337-339),
flow label `ip6_make_flowlabel(…, ip6_autoflowlabel(net, sk), …)` (:341-342)
— hashed with the flow dissector's boot secret unless
**`net.ipv6.auto_flowlabels = 0`** (ipv6.h:938-960: `OFF` returns the
given label, 0). That sysctl is a fixture change (section 6); with it the
IPv6 RST (and every ICMPv6 reply, 2.4.3) has flow label 0 and draws no
`.flowHashSecret`.

So the IPv6 RST reaches `wg_xmit` as `CHECKSUM_PARTIAL` (the mirror's
`skb_checksum_help` decision true, as for the sender's UDP), the IPv4 RST
and all ICMP replies as `CHECKSUM_NONE` (false): a new outcome of an
existing mirror decision, checked by KCOV and by the ciphertext.

#### 2.4.2 ICMPv4 errors

`__icmp_send` (icmp.c:628-830) for port unreachable (udp.c:2524), protocol
unreachable (ip_input.c:216-217), parameter problem (ip_options.c:465-475,
info = the bad byte's offset `<< 24`) and fragment reassembly time exceeded
(ip_fragment.c:184):
- guards: `pkt_type == PACKET_HOST`, not a broadcast/multicast route, only
  fragment 0 (`frag_off & IP_OFFSET`, :684-685), never in reply to an ICMP
  error (:690-716);
- rate limits (2.4.4): `icmpv4_global_allow` unless the input device is
  loopback (:722-728; wg0 is not), then the per-destination
  `icmpv4_xrlim_allow` (:786-788);
- source: `iph->daddr` when the route is local (:738-739) — wg0's address;
  `tos = RT_TOS(iph->tos) | IPTOS_PREC_INTERNETCONTROL` for errors
  (:755-757), i.e. `0xc0 | (inner tos & 0x1e)`; the inner options echoed
  (:760; W6 keeps the bad-option shape to a first bad option, which echoes
  nothing);
- quoted data: `room = min(dst_mtu, 576) − 20 − optlen − 8` = 548,
  `data_len = min(skb_in->len − skb_network_offset(skb_in), room)`
  (:792-805). `skb->len` is the length **at the call**: for UDP, after the
  `ulen` trim (udp.c:2480), so the quote is the IP header plus `ulen`
  bytes, not `tot_len`; for protocol unreachable the whole packet; for
  `ip_expire` the first fragment with its header;
- `icmp_push_reply` (:372-400): the ICMP checksum folded, `CHECKSUM_NONE`;
  `ip_append_data` on the ICMP control socket, whose `pmtudisc` is
  `IP_PMTUDISC_DONT` (:1581): **no DF**, so `__ip_select_ident`
  (route.c:489-507) draws the id: `ip_id_key` (`get_random_bytes` at first
  use, :497, the existing `.ipIdentKey`) and `ip_idents_reserve`
  (:469-487): a `get_random_u32_below(now − old)` when the bucket's jiffies
  stamp moved (:482, the existing `.ipIdentReserve`), **added to a per-bucket
  counter that was random at boot**. The identifier is therefore not
  computable from the records that exist: section 3.3. TTL 64.

#### 2.4.4 Rate limits: three mechanisms

1. **Per-destination token bucket** (`inet_peer_xrlim_allow`,
   inetpeer.c:284-304): `tokens += jiffies − rate_last`, cap
   `6 · timeout`, allow iff `≥ timeout`; a new peer starts with
   `rate_last = jiffies − 60·HZ` (:240-245), i.e. full. IPv4 `timeout =
   sysctl_icmp_ratelimit = HZ` (icmp.c:1545); IPv6 `tmo = icmpv6_time = HZ`
   (af_inet6.c:958) **shifted by the route's prefix length**: the default
   route has `plen 0`, so `tmo >>= 4` = 15 ticks (ipv6/icmp.c:224-225).
   The peer (keyed by the inner source, the reply's destination) is netns
   state: it persists across the traces of a batch, and across the **two
   runs** (PC and comparison mode) of every trace. Its `jiffies` reads are
   not captured; only "certainly allowed" is decidable from the records.
2. **Global credit** (`icmp_global_allow`, icmp.c:233-263): allowed while
   `credit > 0`; a refill (to at most 50, 4 per tick) is attempted only
   when the credit is exhausted and at least `HZ/50` = 5 ticks have passed
   since the last refill stamp (:245-254). `icmp_global_consume` (:266-273)
   draws `get_random_u32_below(3)` — the existing `.icmpCredit` site
   (:268) — once per allowed reply (:334-337, ipv6/icmp.c:233-236).
   `icmpv4_mask_allow` (:276-290) exempts types outside the ratemask
   `0x1818` (:1546); all four reply types here are inside it.
3. **The `ip_error` bucket** (route.c:1021-1034), the same `rate_tokens`
   field, cost `HZ`, burst `5·HZ`: unreachable here (2.4.7).

**Rule [AGENT].** The fixture sets `net.ipv4.icmp_ratelimit = 0` and
`net.ipv6.icmp.ratelimit = 0` once per batch. With `timeout 0`, (1) is
`token = 0; token >= 0` → always allowed (inetpeer.c:296-301), and the
jiffies it reads no longer matter. (2) is always true in the domain: a
false needs 25 or more consumes within 5 ticks of a refill, while the
fixture waits ≥ 100 ticks after every `wg_inject` and no modeled operation
emits more than two errors; the environment asserts that bound
(`unsupported` beyond it) and consumes **exactly one** `.icmpCredit` record
per reply it sends — a tightening of today's `skipCut`. Without the
sysctls the per-source bucket is still decidable with a fresh inner source
per trace and an interval rule (tokens ≥ `1500 − 250·k + 100·(k−1)` after
`k` replies one inject apart, so 7 replies certainly allowed), **but** the
comparison run of the same trace reuses the same source with a depleted
bucket, which the environment cannot see. The sysctl is the clean cut; the
rate-limited arms (`ICMP_MIB_RATELIMITHOST`, a refill) are out of scope
either way (section 9).

#### 2.4.5 Reassembly and `ip_expire`

IPv4: `ip_local_deliver` → `ip_defrag` (ip_fragment.c:471-501) →
`inet_frag_find`/`inet_frag_alloc` (inet_fragment.c:362-412:
`timer_setup(frag_expire)` :376, **`mod_timer(jiffies + fqdir->timeout)`
:410**, `IP_FRAG_TIME = 30·HZ` = 7500 ticks, ip.h:145, ip_fragment.c:679)
→ `ip_frag_queue` (:260-390): a duplicate after completion dropped;
`ip_frag_too_far` (:219-240) on the inetpeer's `rid` — never true with two
fragments; `ecn = 1 << (tos & 3)` OR-ed into `qp->ecn` (:71-74, :346);
`end = offset + len − ihl`; the last fragment fixes `q.len`, a later
fragment beyond it or a mismatched end → `discard_qp` (the queue is
killed); non-last fragments must end on 8 bytes; `pskb_pull` of the
header, `pskb_trim_rcsum` to the declared length (:323-327); overlap
detection in `inet_frag_queue_insert`; `FIRST_IN` at offset 0 (:348-349);
reassembly when `FIRST_IN | LAST_IN` and `meat == len` (:360-366) →
`ip_frag_reasm` (:398-452): `ip_frag_ecn_table[qp->ecn]` (:410;
inet_fragment.c:88-100: CE with ECT → CE; not-ECT mixed with anything →
0xff, **drop**), `tot_len = ihl + q.len` (:433), `tos |= ecn` (:434),
`frag_off` 0 (or DF when every fragment carried DF), `ip_send_check`
(:451). The reassembled skb
keeps `CHECKSUM_UNNECESSARY` (both fragments have it), so UDP delivers it
unverified, trimmed by its `ulen`, or reacts as in 2.2.

Expiry `ip_expire` (:123-193): kills the queue; **only with `FIRST_IN`**
(:154-155) it routes the first fragment again on wg0 (:164-174) and, the
route being local (:179-181), sends `ICMP_TIME_EXCEEDED/EXC_FRAGTIME`
quoting that fragment (:184; the `IP_OFFSET` guard passes, the quote is
the fragment's header plus data, 2.4.2). Without the first fragment the
expiry is silent.

IPv6: `ipv6_frag_rcv` (reassembly.c:325-412; an atomic fragment with
neither `MF` nor an offset is accepted as unfragmented, :350-361),
`fq_find`, `ip6_frag_queue` (the same first/last/end rules, :138-204),
`ip6_frag_reasm`; `IPV6_FRAG_TIMEOUT = 60·HZ` = 15000 ticks (ipv6.h:541,
reassembly.c:540); `ip6_frag_expire` (:74-82) → `ip6frag_expire_frag_queue`
(ipv6_frag.h:65-112): `FIRST_IN` (:94) → `ICMPV6_TIME_EXCEED/EXC_FRAGTIME`
(:108).

**Dispatch.** `ip_expire` and `ip6_frag_expire` are static text symbols;
`dispatch_addresses` accepts `t` (linux.py:63-76), so `DISPATCH_SYMBOLS`
grows to 14 of `KCOV_DISPATCH_MAX` 16 — no kernel change. Once armed,
D3's `kcov_timer_arm` at `mod_timer` (gen-choice-stream.py on `task/d3-int`,
:727-745, :909-914) records kind 303 `{jiffies, expires}` for the queue's
arm at inet_fragment.c:410, and `call_timer_fn` opens a section (kinds
300/301) when it fires. The firing is then captured exactly as a WireGuard
timer's: the environment accepts an `ip_expire` section at an idle point
iff a queue is pending with `expires < J_hi` (D3 section 5.3).

**Netns residue.** A queue that a trace leaves pending fires 30 s or 60 s
later, in some later trace of the batch, as a section the mirror there
cannot explain. Rule [AGENT]: at the end of a trace, a pending fragment
queue is `unsupported`; the generator completes or expires every queue
within its trace (`wg_advance ≥ 7500` or `≥ 15000`, then the wait).

#### 2.4.6 Options and extension headers

IPv4 (`ip_rcv_options`, ip_input.c:260-306): `ip_options_compile` on
error counts `INHDRERRORS`, drops, and **sends a parameter problem**
(ip_options.c:465-475; the pointer is the bad byte's offset; the echo is
empty when no option was parsed before it); a parsed source route is
dropped because `IN_DEV_SOURCE_ROUTE` is off (:286-296); valid padding
(`NOP`/`EOL`, `ihl` 6-15) is delivered. Shapes: padding (delivered; the
UDP arithmetic shifts by `ihl`), a malformed first option (ICMPv4
parameter problem — gated with the ICMPv4 class), LSRR/SSRR (silent drop).
Record-route and timestamp options are parsed and **rewritten in place**
(`ip_options_compile` fills the route's address); without `IP_RECVOPTS`
they are unobserved and stay `unsupported` (section 9).

IPv6: hop-by-hop must be first (ip6_input.c:278-284 → `ipv6_parse_hopopts`,
exthdrs.c:1061-1090) and destination options (:299-330) share `ip6_parse_tlv`
with the unknown-option rule (:65-100): action bits 00 skip, 01 silent
drop, 10 ICMPv6 parameter problem code 2 and drop, 11 the same for a
unicast destination. Pad1/PadN are accepted. A routing header
(`ipv6_rthdr_rcv`, :660+): with `segments_left == 0` it is skipped and the
packet delivered (`looped_back`, :705-730); otherwise, for type 0, the
type switch's default (:746-748) → parameter problem code 0 (`HDR_FIELD`)
with the pointer at the type field; the `hdrlen`/`segments_left` check
(:755-762) applies to the types that reach it (the exact pointer per shape
is fixed by the first trace). An unknown next header →
code 1 (2.4.3). Shapes: hop-by-hop PadN (delivered), destination options
PadN (delivered), an unknown hop-by-hop option under each of the four
action values, routing type 0 with `segments_left` 0 (delivered) and 1
(parameter problem), unknown next header (parameter problem).

#### 2.4.7 `ip_error` host unreachable — deferred, and why

The ICMP arm of `ip_error` (route.c:1003-1038) is reachable only with
`forwarding` on: with it off, every non-local inner destination is dropped
silently (:988-1001; W5 models that drop, 2.2 row 13). Turning forwarding
on changes the behaviour of **every** non-local inner destination — the
default route via wg0 forwards them back out through wg0 (`ip_forward`,
TTL decrement, the hop-limit-exceeded ICMP) — and reaching `ip_error`'s
ICMP then also needs an `unreachable`/`prohibit` route in the fixture.
That is a router-mode fixture with its own shapes, not a receive shape;
it belongs to a later workstream ("wg0 as a router"). The inetpeer bucket
is not the obstacle: it is the same field as 2.4.4's and decidable by the
same means.

### 2.5 A scoped outer source (W5)

`wg_socket_endpoint_from_skb` sets `sin6_scope_id = ipv6_iface_scope_id(
&saddr, skb->skb_iif)` (socket.c:249-250; ipv6.h:571-574: the interface
index iff the address needs a scope — a link-local unicast source), and
`src6 = daddr` (:251). `endpoint_eq` compares the scope (:268), so a roam
between the same address with and without a scope is a real decision;
`send6` uses `flowi6_oif = sin6_scope_id` (:106) and `saddr = src6` (:102),
keeping it iff `ipv6_chk_addr` (:134-135). The dump reports the whole
`sockaddr_in6` (`WGPEER_A_ENDPOINT`), scope included.

Fixture: `lo` has only `::1` (`init_loopback`, addrconf.c:3308-3322); a
link-local `fe80::1/64` is added on `lo` once per batch (no DAD on
`IFF_LOOPBACK`, :4142), and a second sender socket is bound to
`[fe80::1%1]:51997` (a link-local bind needs the scope and sets
`sk_bound_dev_if`, af_inet6.c:350-363). An injection from it reaches wg0's
socket over `lo` with source `fe80::1`, `skb_iif` 1 → the endpoint
`[fe80::1%1]:51997`, `src6 = ::1`. wg0's reply: `send6` with oif 1 and
source `::1` to `fe80::1`, routed by the local route on `lo`
(`RT6_LOOKUP_F_IFACE` for a link-local destination), delivered to the
second socket, whose `recvmsg` reports source `::1`, scope 0. The
environment's "over loopback the source is the destination's own address"
(`Env.deliverSent`) becomes "the source is `endpoint.src6`", which is the
C's own rule. **Verify by one probe trace before the generator relies on
it** (risk R2).

### 2.6 Device tstats (W5)

wg0 is `NETDEV_PCPU_STAT_TSTATS` (device.c:298); `update_rx_stats` adds
`message_data_len(len_before_trim)` to `dev_sw_netstats_rx_add`
(receive.c:19-23; netdevice.h:2763-2771: `rx_bytes += len`,
`rx_packets++`) for every honest packet **including keepalives**
(receive.c:358, `message_data_len(0)` = 32); no transmit counter is ever
added (there is no `dev_sw_netstats_tx_add` in the driver), so
`IFLA_STATS64.tx_packets/tx_bytes` are constant 0. `dev_get_stats` →
`dev_get_tstats64` (dev.c:11092-11093) → `dev_fetch_sw_netstats`
(:11125-11149). The guest already parses `IFLA_STATS64` (guest.c:478-486).

**Does it pay?** With one peer the device's `rx_bytes` equals the peer's
`rx_bytes` in the dump, which is already checked. What the instrument alone
shows: `rx_packets` (a count the dump lacks: a mutant that counts a
keepalive's bytes but not its packet, or two packets for a reassembled
pair), and the counters' persistence after `wg_peer_remove` (W3) when the
peer's own counters vanish from the dump. It costs four fields in the
guest's JSON and two in `Device`. [AGENT] Include it; the stream drops it
if no mutant of section 8 is rejected by it alone.

### 3.1 Captured already (no change)

The random draws on these paths are recorded by the generic `random.c`

`.ipIdentKey` (:497), `.flowHashSecret`; the clock reads WireGuard makes on

`.newHandshakeJitter`; the sections of every armed unit; the kind-303 arm
records for armed functions; the kind-304 advance.

### 3.2 New sites, no kernel change

| Site | Record | Where | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `.inetEhashSecret` | kind 1, 4 bytes | `inet_ehashfn`, net/ipv4/inet_hashtables.c:36 (`net_get_random_once`, inline in the caller as `.flowHashSecret` is) | cut: the first TCP segment per boot (early demux, ip_input.c:339-346, or the lookup); `skipCut` |

`udp_ehashfn`'s secret (udp.c:417) is drawn only for reuseport lookups;
the fixture's sockets are not reuseport.

### 3.3 One new capture: the IPv4 identifier (kernel change, user decision)

`__ip_select_ident` (route.c:489-507) returns `ip_idents[bucket] + delta`:
the delta is recorded, the counter is `get_random_bytes` at boot. The id
is in the ICMPv4 reply's plaintext, under the AEAD, so the ciphertext in
the sender's inbox is not computable without it. Proposal: a generic
record at the source, `kcov_choice_ip(120, _RET_IP_, &id, 2)` after the
id is chosen in `__ip_select_ident`, kind class `ident` in `check.py`,
site `.ipIdent` (the consumer frame — ip.h:587 inlined into
`__ip_make_skb`, ip_output.c:1465 — fixed from the first run, as D1 fixed
`.walltimeLastHandshake`). It is a hidden input recorded where it arises,
like the random draws and clocks. Masking the field in the comparison was
considered and rejected: it would hide the id and the header checksum
behind a decrypt-and-compare step in the evaluator — a comparison-side
mask, fail-open on two fields. Until the capture exists, the ICMPv4 class
is `unsupported` with that reason; ICMPv6 and both RSTs need nothing (the
IPv6 flow label is pinned by the sysctl of 2.4.1, the RST id is 0).

Alternatives that need no kernel change were checked and do not exist: no
sysctl sets a non-DF id to 0; the ICMP socket's `pmtudisc` is fixed at
`DONT` (icmp.c:1581).

### 3.4 Bounded instead of captured

`jiffies` at `icmp_global_allow` (2.4.4, mechanism 2) is bounded by the
fixture's waits; at the inetpeer buckets it is made irrelevant by the
sysctl. Nothing else on these paths reads a hidden input: the fragment
hash table's `hash_rnd` and the inetpeer's creation draw nothing; the
reply routes have no multipath; `ip6_make_flowlabel` returns before
hashing with the sysctl off.

## 4. The environment model

### 4.1 `Leanux/Env/Stack.lean`: the predicate as outcomes

`Env.deliverInner` becomes `Env.Stack.receive (pkt : List UInt8) : M Unit`,
split per family into the arms of 2.2 and 2.3 in Linux's order, each
ending in one of:
- `deliver family src sport bytes tos scope` — appended to the socket's
  inbox with its TOS (the inner header's, after the ECN rewrite) and, for
  IPv6, the source's scope;
- `drop reason` — an enumerated reason (one per table row) and nothing
  else; the trace's `expect` labels may assert it (`stack:drop:<reason>`);
- `react` — one of `Stack.rst4`, `rst6`, `icmp4 type code info`, `icmp6
  type code info`, each a pure function from the received bytes (post-trim
  length and all) to the reply's bytes per 2.4.1-2.4.3, followed by
  `Stack.reply family dst pkt`;
- `unsupported` with the reason, for the rows marked so and for every
  combination the shapes do not cover (options with an ICMP, fragments of
  a reacting packet, a second option after a bad one, ...).

The rewrite of 2.1 belongs to the **mirror** (section 5); the environment
receives the rewritten bytes.

### 4.2 Replies as a nested `wg_xmit`

`Stack.reply` checks the rate rule of 2.4.4 (the per-operation bound; the
sysctl facts are fixture state in `World`), consumes one `.icmpCredit`
record for an ICMP reply (and `.ipIdent` for ICMPv4, 3.3; `.ipIdentKey`
and `.ipIdentReserve` by `skipCut`), then calls the mirror's
`WG.wgXmit family dst pkt` with `pkt.csum := some (40, 16)` for the IPv6
RST and `none` otherwise. It runs inside `rxPoll`'s `napiCompleteDone`,
i.e. inside `runWorker .netRx`, so the encrypt work it queues is picked up
where D2 already picks up the responder's confirmation flush: by
`runCryptWorkers` after the decrypt worker (`Env.inject`), or inside the
decrypt worker's `cond_resched` in order (a) (`Env.condResched`). The
loopback delivery of the ciphertext and the sender's inbox follow. For
`ip_expire`, the reply is queued from a timer section inside `wg_advance`'s
wait and `Env.runTimers` already runs the crypt sections that follow a
fired timer (Env.lean:466-468).

### 4.3 Fragment queues and the expiry units

`World.fragQueues : List FragQueue` with `{family, key (src, dst, id,
proto), fragments (offset, end, bytes, ecn), len?, firstIn, lastIn,
expires, iif}`. On a fragment: `fragTimerArm` consumed on a new queue
(`expires` from the record, `jiffies` noted as `jiffiesLast`), then the
rules of 2.4.5; on completion the reassembled packet goes back through
`Stack.receive` (minus the fragment arm). Two new environment units,
`Worker.fragExpire4` ("ip_expire") and `.fragExpire6` ("ip6_frag_expire"),
section ids after D3's; `Env.runTimers` handles their sections before
`fireTimer`: exactly one queue with `expires < J_hi` (several →
`unsupported`, their order), then the expiry: silent without `firstIn`,
else `icmp4/6 time-exceeded` quoting the first fragment, via
`Stack.reply`. The trace-end rule of 2.4.5 lives in `Runner.executeTrace`'s
end-of-trace check next to the pending-stops check. Without records (the
domain filter) `runTimersPlaceholder` fires due queues like timers.

### 4.4 Other state

`World.senderTos`, per op (section 6); `World.senderLinkLocalInbox` for
the second socket, with `isSender` extended; `Device.rxPackets`,
`Device.rxBytes` (2.6); `World.icmpSentThisOp` for the rule of 2.4.4;
`World.fixture` facts (`autoFlowlabelsOff`, `icmpRatelimitOff`,
`loLinkLocal`) asserted by the guest's provenance so a trace run against a
fixture without them is an environment failure, not a silent change of
verdicts.

## 10. Risks

- **R1 (medium): environment facts verified only by the first trace** —
  the ICMPv6 traffic class and hop limit, the routing-header pointers, the
  ehash-secret consumer frames, the `.ipIdent` frame, the broadcast and
  link-local extras. Each fails noisily (bytes, or an undeclared consumer)
  and is fixed once; the generator does not rely on a fact before its
  corpus trace passes.
- **R2 (medium): the scoped-source path** (2.5) crosses several stack
  rules (link-local bind, `RT6_LOOKUP_F_IFACE`, the reply's source `::1`).
  A probe trace first; if the reply is not delivered to the second socket
  as predicted, the shape is reduced to "the endpoint roams and the dump
  shows the scope" with the reply `unsupported`.
- **R3 (low): netns residue across traces and the two runs** — inetpeers
  (pinned off), the global credit (bounded), fragment queues (the trace-end

- **R4 (low): KCOV budget and `DISPATCH_MAX`** — 14 of 16 symbols; a

## 12. Decisions and open questions for the user

- **D1 (kernel change): the IPv4 identifier capture** (3.3) — kind 120 at
  `__ip_select_ident`. Without it the ICMPv4 class (port/protocol
  unreachable, parameter problem, fragment time exceeded) stays
  `unsupported`; everything else in W5/W6 proceeds. Approve, or defer the
  ICMPv4 class.
- **D2 (oracle configuration): three fixture facts** — `net.ipv6.
  auto_flowlabels = 0` (needed for any IPv6 reply: the flow label is
  otherwise hashed with a boot secret the mirror cannot know),
  `net.ipv4.icmp_ratelimit = 0` and `net.ipv6.icmp.ratelimit = 0` (2.4.4:
  the per-destination bucket's `jiffies` are otherwise unobservable, and
  the comparison run reuses the source). They are set once per batch,
  checked and recorded in the provenance like the clocksource. The
  alternative for the rate limits — default sysctls, a fresh source per
  trace, an interval rule, and accepting that the second run of a trace is
  undecidable after a few replies — is weaker; the alternative for the
  flow label does not exist.
- **D3: `ip_error` deferred** to a router-mode workstream (2.4.7). Agree?
- **D4: tstats** — include per 2.6, or drop as not paying.
- **Q1:** should `wg_inject`/`wg_xmit`'s TOS be a per-op field (proposed:
  deterministic, no fixture state) or a separate `wg_sender_tos` operation?
- **Q2:** the generator's catch-all allowed IP (`0.0.0.0/0`, `::/0`) for
  the drop shapes changes `lookup_src`'s trie shape in those traces only;
  fine as a per-class choice, unless the user wants the fixture's
  `10.0.0.0/8` kept everywhere (then the reachable stack drops shrink to
  the local source).
- **Q3:** the cheap extras (IPv4 broadcast delivery, an inner link-local
  IPv6 source with its scope in `recvmsg`) — include in R after a probe,
  or leave listed.
